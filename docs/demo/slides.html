The problem: two satellites, two different counts

  • MODIS (2000 →) detects fires with 1 km pixels
  • VIIRS (2012 →) has 375 m pixels and finds many more small fires
  • Joined naively, fire activity seems to jump in 2012: a change of instrument, not of burning
  • MODIS is near the end of its mission, so the record has to continue on VIIRS

So it's hard to answer a simple question:
“Is this month unusual for this place?”

FireCalendar India2

Our approach: count cells, then calibrate

1 · FilterNASA FIRMS detections for India. Vegetation fires only; MODIS confidence ≥ 30, VIIRS nominal/high.
→
2 · GridEach detection goes into an H3 cell (≈ 5 km²). Unit: the fire cell-day, which removes most of the pixel-size gap.
→
3 · Calibratek = VIIRS ÷ MODIS cell-days on the 2012–2025 overlap, per 2° block × month × day/night.
→
4 · ServeOne series in VIIRS units, anomalies vs the median, for any area you draw.
  • Aqua MODIS and Suomi NPP VIIRS both pass at 13:30, so their counts compare fairly
  • Sparse blocks are pulled toward the national ratio; every factor has a 90% bootstrap interval
  • Terra (2000–02) and NOAA-20 (2026 →) are chained in with their own ratios

Who it helps

  • Fire & disaster managers: is this month unusual? When does the season start here?
  • Scientists: a consistent 2000–present series, with raw counts and calibration factors kept
  • Land & air-quality managers: e.g. Punjab's Oct–Nov paddy-residue burning, year by year

Open & accessible: colourblind-safe colours, every number printed, table views, CSV + PNG downloads, works on a phone.
